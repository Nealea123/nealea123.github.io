## Introduction
In embodied robotic manipulation, imitation learning (IL) and reinforcement learning (RL) are mainstream policy acquisition paradigms with distinct strengths: IL enables sample-efficient policy learning from human demonstrations without manual programming, while RL supports autonomous exploration in dynamic environments to outperform original demonstrations. Nevertheless, IL and RL have inherent limitations and lack complementary integration: IL fails to generalize due to distribution mismatch, error accumulation, expert data reliance, and poor adaptability to contact-rich fine-grained tasks, while RL faces inefficient exploration and high trial-and-error costs, only partially mitigated by IL based pre-training. To address these challenges, we propose a bionic mix-of-experts (MoE) framework that dynamically assigns high-determinism coarse motions to IL and high-uncertainty contact-rich fine manipulations to RL. In the offline phase, our method independently trains the IL and RL modules to extract policies from limited demonstrations and stabilize value estimation; in the online phase, we continue their training and leverage the MoE algorithm for efficient policy selection, thus reducing manual intervention and accelerating task convergence. We evaluated our method on six complex manipulation tasks in simulation and real-world environments; within 2-4 hours of online fine-tuning, it achieved a 98\% average success rate, outperforming prior supervised and reinforcement learning methods, and reduced convergence time and manual intervention by 50\% each while doubling the proportion of successful training data. This work highlights the potential of integrating reinforcement learning and imitation learning via MoE to improve performance in robotic applications.
